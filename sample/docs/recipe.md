# Seeded Rye and Buttermilk Loaf

![A dark rye loaf, sliced, on a wooden board](../assets/rye-loaf.png)

A no-knead overnight loaf. Ten minutes of work spread across two days, most of it waiting. Forgiving about timing, unforgiving about oven temperature.

**Makes:** one 900 g loaf · **Active:** 20 min · **Total:** 18 h · **Difficulty:** easy

---

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume | Notes |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | :--- |
| Dark rye flour | 250 g | ~2 cups | Wholegrain, not light rye |
| Strong white flour | 250 g | ~2 cups | 12%+ protein |
| Buttermilk | 380 g | 1½ cups | Room temperature |
| Water | 60 g | ¼ cup | Lukewarm |
| Fine sea salt | 10 g | 1¾ tsp | |
| Instant yeast | 3 g | ¾ tsp | Or 9 g fresh |
| Blackstrap molasses | 20 g | 1 tbsp | Optional, for colour |
| Caraway seeds | 8 g | 1½ tsp | Toasted |
| Sunflower seeds | 60 g | ⅓ cup | Plus more for the top |
| Flaked sea salt | — | to finish | |

> 💡 **Substitutions:** Kefir or thinned yoghurt work in place of buttermilk. Skip the molasses and the loaf is paler and slightly less sweet — no other change. Caraway is the flavour most people either love or want gone; leaving it out is fine.

## Method

### Day one — evening (10 minutes)

1. Toast the caraway and sunflower seeds in a dry pan over medium heat until they smell nutty, about 3 minutes. Tip onto a plate to cool — they will keep cooking in the pan.

2. Whisk the flours, salt, and yeast in a large bowl. Add the cooled seeds.

3. Stir the molasses into the buttermilk and water until it dissolves, then pour into the dry ingredients.

4. Mix with a spoon or one hand until no dry flour remains. It will be sticky, shaggy, and unpromising. **Do not knead it.**

5. Cover and leave at room temperature (18–22 °C) for **12 to 16 hours**.

### Day two — morning (10 minutes plus baking)

6. The dough should have roughly doubled and be covered in bubbles. Scrape it onto a well-floured surface — rye is stickier than wheat, so use more flour than feels reasonable.

7. Fold it over itself four or five times into a rough round. Do not degas it more than you have to.

8. Place seam-side down on parchment, scatter sunflower seeds and flaked salt over the top, and cover with a tea towel. Rest **1 hour**.

9. Meanwhile, put a lidded cast-iron pot in the oven and heat to **240 °C / 465 °F** for at least 45 minutes. The pot must be properly hot; this is the step people rush.

10. Lower the loaf in on its parchment, lid on. Bake **25 minutes covered**, then **20–25 minutes uncovered** until deep brown and hollow-sounding.

11. Cool on a rack for **at least 2 hours** before slicing. Rye sets as it cools, and cutting it warm gives you a gummy crumb — this is not optional the way step 10's timing is.

## Timings at a glance

| Step | Duration | Can you leave? |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| Mix | 10 min | — |
| Bulk ferment | 12–16 h | Yes |
| Shape and rest | 1 h | Yes |
| Preheat pot | 45 min | Yes |
| Bake covered | 25 min | Yes |
| Bake uncovered | 20–25 min | Watch the last 5 |
| Cool | 2 h+ | Yes |

## Troubleshooting

- **Dense, wet crumb.** Under-baked or cut too early. Give it the full uncovered bake and the full cool.
- **No rise overnight.** Kitchen below 16 °C, or dead yeast. Move somewhere warmer and add 2 hours.
- **Spreads flat when shaped.** Over-fermented — 16 hours was too long for your kitchen. Try 12.
- **Bitter aftertaste.** Burnt caraway. Toast it more gently next time.

## Keeping

Cut-side down on a board for two days. After that, slice the whole loaf and freeze it flat in a bag; it toasts from frozen without thawing. Do not refrigerate — it stales faster there than on the counter.
